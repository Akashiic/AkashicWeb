# Codecs, live streams, relay, latency and rights

> Research stream `05-codec-path`, produced 2026-10-05..10 by a research agent in a Linux cloud container. Raw output: [`raw/05-codec-path.json`](raw/05-codec-path.json). Paths such as `/home/user/...` or `scratchpad/...` refer to that container and do not exist in this repository; the reusable probe sources are under [`/spikes`](../../spikes).

Why YouTube live fails on stock CEF, the codec-enabled OBS CEF build (built and run on Linux), the Akashic relay measurements (WebRTC/LL-HLS), latency budget per path, and rights (YouTube ToS, Lei 9.610/98).

> **Note (2026-10-11):** the relay and re-streaming parts of this stream were abandoned by the owner (DECISIONS D-008). What still matters here: YouTube live formats, the OBS codec-enabled CEF build, latency and rights.

## Summary

I picked up where the earlier run stopped and finished the task. Two results change the earlier picture, and both are backed by tests run in this container.

(1) The earlier note that CinemaMod's CEF 116 ships without proprietary codecs is wrong. Its MCEF natives (java-cef eaeb3d4, Chromium 116.0.5845.190) played an H.264+AAC MP4 at runtime, and MSE reported avc1 and mp4a as supported. The Windows libcef.dll contains the same Chrome-only codec markers.

(2) OBS Studio pins and hosts its own CEF builds with codecs on cdn-fastly.obsproject.com. The current one is CEF 150.0.19/150.0.20 on Chromium 150.0.7871.252, for Windows, Linux and macOS. They are standard CEF binary distributions, so java-cef builds against them. I built CCBlueX java-cef against the OBS Linux build in about 5 minutes; the only change was deleting 6 lines for 3 network error codes that CEF 150 does not have. The result plays H.264/AAC, MPEG-TS in MSE, native HLS, and WebRTC with VP8, VP9, H.264 and AV1.

Path A (YouTube live in a stock, no-codec CEF) is a dead end. yt-dlp issue tables show live audio is AAC only (itags 139 and 140, no Opus) and live HLS video is H.264. VP9 exists only as video-only DASH. Players in no-codec MCEF/WebDisplays have reported for years that YouTube live will not play.

Path C (building CEF ourselves) does not fit the deadline. It needs 16–32 GB RAM, 120–150 GB disk and about 4 hours per platform. GitHub-hosted runners have 14 GB disk and a 6-hour job limit. OBS already ships the same result.

Path D (the Akashic relay) is the most reliable option. I measured it end to end on localhost inside the JCEF/Chromium 150 off-screen browser, with VP9 720p30 + Opus through MediaMTX v1.21.1:
- LL-HLS with hls.js 1.7.3: median 1.04 s; two clients at the same time showed 0.92 s and 1.03 s.
- WebRTC (WHEP): median 126 ms; two clients showed 127 ms and 149 ms.

Realtime encoding is cheap on a 4-vCPU Xeon: VP9 720p30 used 0.40 of a core, 480p 0.26, VP8 720p30 0.57, and SVT-AV1 preset 12 about 0.56. Each viewer needs about 1.8–2.0 Mbit/s at 720p, so the relay must fan out from a well-connected server, not the owner's home connection. OBS can send AV1+Opus over WHIP directly to MediaMTX, which removes the transcode step entirely. Chromium decodes AV1 (dav1d) and Opus without proprietary codecs, but it has no H.264 in WebRTC without them.

Latency, answering the owner directly: the image cannot be "almost 0 ms" behind the real event. The broadcaster and YouTube already add delay (Google: under 10 s for low latency, under 5 s for ultra-low; no number for normal). What can be close to zero is the difference between players: tens of ms with WebRTC, or under about 100 ms with LL-HLS plus server-clock steering. That steering uses MediaMTX's EXT-X-PROGRAM-DATE-TIME, hls.playingDate, and AkashicFM's existing ServerClock ping-pong. Our relay adds roughly 0.3–0.6 s with WebRTC or 1.3–2.5 s with LL-HLS over the internet (estimates). His own restream adds his platform's delay on top, and YouTube Content ID scans live streams for other broadcasts and can interrupt them.

Rights: YouTube allows the official embed but forbids downloading or restreaming. Brazilian copyright law (Lei 9.610/98, art. 95) gives broadcasters exclusive control of retransmission and public showing. TV Câmara publishes legislative footage under CC BY 4.0 and other content under CC BY-NC-ND; TV Senado allows non-commercial retransmission with credit.

Ranking: D first (about 85–90% chance, medium effort). B second as backup and for direct YouTube embeds (about 70%, medium effort, codec licensing is a grey area). CinemaMod 116 only as an emergency fallback (about 50%; old Chromium and a different java-cef API). C rejected (about 30%, high effort). A rejected (about 2%). The labelled last-resort fallback is the in-game browser showing TSE's official results web page.

Not run: anything on Windows or macOS, any test over the real internet, anything against YouTube (this IP is bot-walled), and any load test. The stock no-codec jcefmaven 152 natives would not start in my harness (ContentMainRun exit code 28), so WebRTC AV1/VP9 on the no-codec build is still a static inference, not a runtime result. All builds and patches were made only in scratch copies; no repository was modified.
